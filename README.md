# Bonus Tax Calculator UK — How Much Tax Will I Pay on My Bonus?

> Of a £5,000 bonus you keep £3,600.00 on a £40,000 salary, but only £1,900.00 on £100,000.

Reference notes for the UK tax year **2026/27**, computed from HMRC's
published rates rather than copied from another site.

## How it works

There is no bonus tax rate. A bonus is ordinary earnings at your marginal rate.

| Base salary | Kept from a £5,000 bonus | Lost |
|---|---:|---:|
| £40,000 | £3,600.00 | 28.0% |
| £50,000 | £2,937.80 | 41.2% |
| £100,000 | £1,900.00 | 62% |

The last row is the personal allowance taper: 60% effective plus 2% NI.

Two separate things make the payslip look worse than this. Cumulative PAYE briefly treats the bonus as your new normal and over-deducts, then corrects itself. National Insurance is charged per period and does **not** correct itself.

## What this does not cover

Bonus sacrifice into a pension must be agreed **before** you become entitled to the money. After it is paid it is too late.

## Source and tax year

Figures are for the 2026/27 tax year, which runs 6 April 2026
to 5 April 2027, and were checked against
[gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/rates-and-thresholds-for-employers-2026-to-2027) on 28 August 2026.

Rates change every April, and often at a Budget. Check the tax year a figure
belongs to before relying on it.
